# Inspiration

Read this before writing concept cards (references/concepts.md). It holds the principles behind top store
screenshots and a library of real sets, each broken down into what makes it work. **Take the principle, never the
look.** No concept may be recognisable as a copy of any set below.

To *see* a reference, download it into the scratchpad and view it (`curl -sL URL -o …/ref.jpg`, then Read the
file). Pick the 2 to 4 closest to this app's category and mood. The images belong to their designers: never copy
them into the project or the skill.

## Seven principles (Scrnsht Studio playbook)

Source: https://x.com/scrnshtstudio/status/2090816024585089129 ("How to Make Your App Store Screenshots Convert").

1. **Slide 1 is the value prop.** The simpler and clearer, the better. It comes from the users' pain and how the
   app differs, so research first and design second.
2. **Sell the outcome, not the feature.** Nobody wants a calorie tracker; they want to lose weight. Ask what the
   user hopes to achieve, and make every slide reinforce that one outcome.
3. **One screenshot, one message.** Secondary information may support it, but one idea must stand out.
4. **Make the idea obvious.** People look before they read. Find the visual hook before the headline. Test: hide
   every word. If the slide still says roughly what the app does, it works.
5. **Humanize.** A face, a hand or a real situation catches the eye faster than another UI mockup. Use people when
   they make sense (only photos the user owns or licenses).
6. **Build trust.** Ratings, reviews, awards, downloads, press, trusted brands. Never fake them; honesty is a trust
   signal too.
7. **Keep copy simple.** The shortest copy wins, and short copy can be set huge. Use the users' own words.

### What the library agrees on

- **Colour comes from the brand**, used as a system: one hue in many tones, or a neutral canvas with the brand
  only on the key word and UI, or brand / white / dark slides in rhythm. Extra colour comes from the app's own
  content (posters, food, drawings, photos), not from a stock accent.
- **3D is a seasoning.** Most slides have none. When it appears it is either big (a mascot or hero object, often
  cropped by the edge or crossing slides) or small and inline (a chip in the headline, an emoji). Its style matches
  the mood: toy-like for games, plush for calm, clay for friendly, photo-real for real-world subjects.
- **Proof is everywhere and small.** A laurel with a real number, a rating, a press mark, a quote. Usually on
  slide 1 under or above the headline, or in a bottom band.
- **Headlines are short and huge.** One idea, often the outcome or a question in the user's words.
- **The app's own content can be the art.** Covers, recurrence rules, cards, posters: enlarged and arranged, with
  no phone at all on that slide.
- **One element leaves the phone.** The card, notification or number that matters is lifted out and enlarged, so
  the eye lands there first.

### Prop and effect references the client pointed at (described, not linked)

- **3D props**: glossy, rounded "3D icon" e-commerce objects (bag, box, cart, card, truck) in the 3dicons.co style.
- **Laurel award**: thin hand-drawn style laurel branches around 2 short lines, used "marketing style so it feels
  like an award-winning, legendary something", with generic, store-safe wording.
- **Cart**: a chunky toy-like 3D cart with an empty basket, the body in a shade of the base colour and the handle
  in the accent.
- **Truck**: a toy box-truck with a rounded cab and a big front face (not a flat-nosed van), plus short speed bars.
- **Globe**: a clay/plasticine earth with soft relief, green land and blue water, with parcels and small pins
  anchored to its surface (pins point at the globe centre). Keep it sharp, and keep at least one pin near the top.
- **Ribbon**: a glossy satin band wrapping around the phone (behind, then in front), with a natural twist and no
  hard folds.
- **Motion trail**: 3–4 short, glossy, rounded bars of varied length behind a moving object, all in one direction.
- **Tag**: a price-tag silhouette with a punched hole and a bolt icon, the hole end tucked under the phone frame.
