# Copywriting

## Rules

- **Headline**: 2–3 lines, 2–4 words a line, about 9 words max. One idea. Benefit or outcome, not a feature name.
  Rhythm patterns that worked: "Every aisle. / Every seller. / One search."; "Fewer taps. / More orders.";
  "Turn viewers / into buyers."; "Your store, / in every / pocket."
- **Subtitle**: one plain sentence, 4–9 words, a concrete benefit. Start with the noun or verb, not "An"/"The"
  ("Easy cart and checkout that turns browsers into buyers.").
- **No em dashes (—) or en dashes (–)**, ever. Use periods and commas. `studio check` flags them.
- **No unverifiable claims**: #1, best, leading, award-winning, guaranteed, "loved by millions". Numbers must be real
  and the client must confirm them. This is store-guideline safety (Apple 2.3.7 / Google Play metadata policy).
- **No prices, "free", discounts or rankings** unless the client confirms they're accurate and permanent.
- **Do not repeat the highlighted word** across slides (if slide 6 highlights "trust", slide 4 can't use "trust").
- **Speak to the actual reader.** For a white-label or demo app sold to businesses: "your store", "your buyers",
  "launch your own branded app". For a consumer app: "you", "your".
- **Brand relationships, exactly right**: for sister products say "Acme is made by ParentCo, the team behind
  Rocket", never "built by the Rocket team". Ask who owns what.
- **Brand names keep their casing**, even in all-caps archetypes: wrap them in `<span style="text-transform:none">bKash</span>`
  (or pick a slide headline without them).
- **Easy words.** A 12-year-old understands every word. No jargon ("omnichannel", "seamless", "leverage",
  "elevate", "unlock", "empower"). No AI clichés.

## Giving options (the client chooses)

Present 3–5 options per slot with AskUserQuestion (multiSelect when they pick several, such as two laurel claims).
Recommended option first. Keep the label short and put the full line in the description. Generate options that
genuinely differ in angle (outcome / feeling / proof / speed), not synonyms.

## Laurel and seal claims (generic, store-safe)

Laurels: "Your brand, / your app" · "Built to / sell more" · "Made for / every store" · "Ready / on day one".
Seal ring text: "ALWAYS OPEN • NEVER CLOSED •", "VERIFIED SELLER • TRUSTED STORE •", "STORES • MARKETPLACES •
BRANDS •". Seal centre: "Shop 24/7", "Built for scale", or a check icon.
Stat: only a positive, true, client-confirmed number (never "0 …" or a workaround). Stat label: what the number *means* for the reader ("MARKETPLACES TRUST OUR TECH"), never just "users".
